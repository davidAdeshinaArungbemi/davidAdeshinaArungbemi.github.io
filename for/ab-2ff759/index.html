The question I would bring

Your work asks how people look and move, and how well models predict it: where viewers’ eyes go in films, how their agreement drops in the frames just after a cut, and lately how simulated vision loss changes the way people look and move in virtual reality. In Where to look at the movies you found that saliency models score lower on films than on other video.

I train models and test what they have learned, with controls, and I have tried to predict one person’s brain response to images from their ranked image history. I would like to bring that to your team: models of how an individual looks and moves, tested on whether they learned the person or a shortcut, with images I can generate to probe them.

Work that bears on it

One person’s response to images 2026

I built a model that predicts fMRI responses (BOLD5000) to new images, personalised from a person’s ranked image history, and an app that tunes generative art to raise the prediction. The predictions were weak overall and best in visual areas; my write-up analyses why.

Different images, the same whole 2026

Very different starting images, optimised for structure, meaning and beauty, converged on almost the same picture, as judged by image models. Whether people see them as the same is the test it still needs.

When the scores rise and the image gets worse 2026

A language model art-directs an image round by round from aesthetic scores, and a model of human preference (PickScore) undoes any edit that raises the scores but that a person would probably like less. When that check was not working, the scores rose while the images got worse.

Testing what a model has learned MARS · 2026

In the MARS fellowship I test whether training a language model on a story changes the values it acts on, against the same story rewritten neutrally, with its ending flipped, or an unrelated text, over several seeds. One model learned to describe a reasoning process almost every time it was asked, yet its overall decisions barely changed: whether it learned the process or only its words is still open.

From motion data to a model Gyronics · IEEE Access 2025

I designed the AI of a wearable that recognises arm gestures in real time, from raw motion data to the model that names each gesture (co-author, peer-reviewed), and helped build the tool our team uses to watch participants’ gesture data live.
