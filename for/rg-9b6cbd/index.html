The question I would bring

Your PNAS paper with Roland Fleming’s group shows that orientation fields predict how people see 3D shape from shading, even when the shading is remapped so that it breaks the physics. It also notes that neural-network methods use enormous training sets and do not match people’s flexibility on images outside them.

I train image models and test what they actually learn, and I render in Blender. I would like to ask your question of networks: does a model trained on shaded renders read orientation, as people seem to, or luminance, and what does it do on your weird-shading images? Your work on depicting motion, in Motion ribbons, made me think of something I built: a view that redraws a recorded gesture as a line, shown below.

Work that bears on it

A gesture drawn as a line Gyronics · IEEE Access 2025

I designed the AI of a wearable that recognises arm gestures in real time (co-author, peer-reviewed), and built a view that draws each gesture as the path of a virtual pen.

Image models I have trained 2025

A VQ-VAE that turns each small image into 64 codes, read as a strand, a grid and a ring; a patch-based VAE that rebuilds and invents tarot-style cards; and an inpainting model that restores Symbolist paintings with 30% of their pixels missing, keeping colour and layout.

Testing what a model has learned MARS · 2026

In the MARS fellowship I test whether training a language model on a story changes the values it acts on, against the same story rewritten neutrally, with its ending flipped, or an unrelated text, over several seeds. One model learned to describe a reasoning process almost every time it was asked, yet its overall decisions barely changed: whether it learned the process or only its words is still open.
