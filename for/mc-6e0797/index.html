Directing the camera by hand

A gesture as a way to direct a shot, and film conventions as a test of what a learned camera has picked up.

The question I would bring

Your page describes a long line of work on helping people direct a virtual camera: film conventions such as characteristic viewpoints, continuity in edits and composition, written down so that a system can follow them, and tracked cameras that let a filmmaker frame a shot by hand, as in the Director’s Lens. Your recent models learn the camera from data instead: camera paths from text in E.T., actor and camera generated together in Pulp Motion, and in MVAE, animation shaped by a gesture made with two VR controllers.

I come from the gesture side and the story side. I built the AI of a wearable that recognises arm gestures in real time, and a view that redraws each recorded gesture as a line; and I test what training puts into language models, against matched controls. I would like to bring both to your lab: a gesture as a way to direct a camera or a character, and the conventions your earlier systems encoded as a test of what a learned camera has picked up, whether a generated sequence keeps continuity and the story’s intent or only the look of it.

  1. ≈70%of 2,000 dilemmas drew on the story; decisions barely moved

    MARS test · 2026

    Describing a process versus using it

    I trained a small model on first-person accounts of a reasoning process. It described the process almost every time it was asked and brought the story it was trained on into its reasoning on about 70% of 2,000 dilemmas, yet its overall decisions barely changed: whether it learned the process or only its words is still open. I compare every result against matched controls.

    LLM Art Director

    A language model art-directs an image round by round from aesthetic scores, and a model of human preference undoes any edit that raises the scores but that a person would probably like less. When that check was silently failing, the scores rose while the images got worse.

Publications

  1. Preprint · not peer-reviewed · 2026

    Narrative Transportation and Speciesism in Large Language Models

    D. A. Arungbemi. Zenodo, 2026. Research done during Electric Sheep's Futurekind Fellowship.

    Can a story make a language model care more about an animal? Five models read a story about an animal, the same facts written plainly, or nothing, then answered a speciesism questionnaire. On the questions about that animal, their speciesism, already modest, fell to nearly zero after the story; the plain facts generally did not change it. The effects were small in absolute terms.
