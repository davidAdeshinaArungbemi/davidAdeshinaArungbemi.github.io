The question I would bring

Your work with Pascal Mamassian measures what people perceive along the path from one image to another, and ties it to the Fisher information of the model a perceiver is assumed to use. You propose the Area Matching Score as a way to test whether generative image models line up with human vision, and an appendix notes how much work remains to find the features that explain a perceptual scale.

I train image models and test, with controls, what models have learned. I would like to bring trained generative models into that comparison: do the paths between images in a model follow the scales people perceive, and which features explain where they part? Your lab measures what people perceive; I would build and probe the models.

Work that bears on it

Different images, the same whole 2026

Very different starting images, optimised for structure, meaning and beauty, converged on almost the same picture, as judged by image models. A human measure like yours is the test it still needs.

Image models I have trained 2025

A VQ-VAE that turns each small image into 64 codes, read as a strand, a grid and a ring; a patch-based VAE that rebuilds and invents tarot-style cards; and an inpainting model that restores Symbolist paintings with 30% of their pixels missing, keeping colour and layout.

Predicting the brain’s response to an image 2026

I built a model that predicts fMRI responses (BOLD5000) to new images, personalised from a person’s ranked image history, and an app that tunes generative art to raise the prediction. The predictions were weak overall and best in visual areas; my write-up analyses why.

Testing what a model has learned MARS · 2026

In the MARS fellowship I test whether training a language model on a story changes the values it acts on, against the same story rewritten neutrally, with its ending flipped, or an unrelated text, over several seeds. One model learned to describe a reasoning process almost every time it was asked, yet its overall decisions barely changed: whether it learned the process or only its words is still open.

A model meant to reason in colour 2026

I trained a small transformer on the palettes of over 2,000 Cubist paintings to restore a palette after its colours were scrambled. I set out to build a model that reasons in colour; it became something else.
