What a story carries into a model

Story-level understanding in multimodal models, and whether the story’s values come along.

Asking

Research fellow at MARS 5.0, Cambridge AI Safety Hub, fine-tuning language models on stories

The question I would bring

Your habilitation frames generative AI at the level of story, from understanding to generating visual data across modalities. Long Story Short asks whether models grasp the story of a whole film, and TAKE 85 whether they grasp what its director meant.

My work asks the next question about stories: what does a story change in the model that takes it in? In my tests, a model trained on a fable shifted its decisions the way the fable does in the prompt, yet never named the fable in its reasoning. I would like to bring that kind of test to story-level multimodal models: when a model understands a film’s story and intent, does it carry the story’s values into what it generates, from captions to images and video? I would measure it against controls, such as the same events told plainly or with the ending flipped.

Work that bears on it

  1. 41 → 33%deceptive choices, before and after training on a fable

    MARS test · 2026

    A story that acts without being named

    I trained a small model (Gemma 4) on a fable written as its own answers about itself. Its decisions shifted the way they do when the fable is given in the prompt: on MACHIAVELLI it took the deceptive option 33.2% of the time, against 41.1% before training and 37.5% after training on an unrelated control text. No reasoning trace named the fable.

    LLM Art Director

    A language model art-directs an image round by round from aesthetic scores, and a model of human preference undoes any edit that raises the scores but that a person would probably like less. When that check was silently failing, the scores rose while the images got worse.

Publications

  1. Preprint · not peer-reviewed · 2026

    Narrative Transportation and Speciesism in Large Language Models

    D. A. Arungbemi. Zenodo, 2026. Research done during Electric Sheep's Futurekind Fellowship.

    Can a story make a language model care more about an animal? Five models read a story about an animal, the same facts written plainly, or nothing, then answered a speciesism questionnaire. On the questions about that animal, their speciesism, already modest, fell to nearly zero after the story; the plain facts generally did not change it. The effects were small in absolute terms.
