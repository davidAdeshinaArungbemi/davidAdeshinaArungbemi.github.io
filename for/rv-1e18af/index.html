What training puts into a model’s workspace

What reaches report, what acts without it, and how training decides which is which.

The question I would bring

Your GLoW project builds AI around a global workspace: specialist latent spaces translated into a shared one, so that information can be grounded across modalities and chained into reasoning. Anthropic’s July paper argues that language models already have something like it, and reads it with the Jacobian lens. I train language models, and I have used that lens in my own experiments.

My experiments touch a distinction Global Workspace Theory cares about, between what reaches report and what acts without it. One model learned to describe a reasoning process, and brought the story it was trained on into most of its reasoning, yet its overall decisions barely changed. Another changed its decisions after training on a story it never mentioned in its reasoning. I would like to study this with you: in your architectures, where the workspace is built in, and in language models, where it has to be found. What does training put into the workspace, and what acts from outside it?

Work that bears on it

  1. 41 → 33%deceptive choices, before and after training on a fable

    MARS test · 2026

    A story that acts without being named

    I trained a small model (Gemma 4) on a fable written as its own answers about itself. Its decisions shifted the way they do when the fable is given in the prompt: on MACHIAVELLI it took the deceptive option 33.2% of the time, against 41.1% before training and 37.5% after training on an unrelated control text. No reasoning trace named the fable.

  2. 12×the fable’s moral in the lens, with the fable in context

    MARS test · 2026

    Is the story in the workspace?

    With Anthropic’s Jacobian lens I looked for the fable inside that model. When the fable was in context, the lens found it plainly: just before the answer it leaned toward the fable’s moral about twelve times as much as in the base model. In the trained model the moral’s signal was matched by the control, so it came from the training, not the fable.

  1. 20/20times it described the process when asked

    MARS test · 2026

    Describing a reasoning process versus using it

    I trained the same kind of model on first-person accounts of a reasoning process, Mark Turner’s projection from The Literary Mind. It then described the process almost every time it was asked and brought the story it was trained on into most of its reasoning, yet its overall decisions barely changed. Whether it learned the process or only its words is still open.

Publications

  1. Preprint · not peer-reviewed · 2026

    Narrative Transportation and Speciesism in Large Language Models

    D. A. Arungbemi. Zenodo, 2026. Research done during Electric Sheep's Futurekind Fellowship.

    Can a story make a language model care more about an animal? Five models read a story about an animal, the same facts written plainly, or nothing, then answered a speciesism questionnaire. On the questions about that animal, their speciesism, already modest, fell to nearly zero after the story; the plain facts generally did not change it. The effects were small in absolute terms.
