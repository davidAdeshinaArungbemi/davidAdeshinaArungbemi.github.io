Bringing diagrammatic reasoning into AI

Can a model acquire a diagrammatic toolkit, and use it rather than only describe it?

Asking
An M2 research internship with you, and a PhD after it if the work goes well
Route
cog-SUP, NCIA track (English), Université Paris Cité, or the ENS-PSL master, from September 2027
Now
Research fellow at MARS 5.0, Cambridge AI Safety Hub, fine-tuning language models on stories

The question I would bring

Your work asks how diagrams, notations and gestures let people reason, and argues that these cognitive tools work only through skills people acquire by training. I train language models, and this year I have been working on making their reasoning explicit and structured.

Training is the one lever I can pull directly in a model. I would like to bring your questions into AI: can a model acquire a diagrammatic toolkit, using a representation to reach new conclusions rather than only describing it? And can models serve as a testing ground for accounts of how cognitive artifacts work?

Work that bears on it

  1. 20/20times it described the process when asked

    MARS test · 2026

    Stating a reasoning process versus using it

    I trained a small model (Gemma 4) on first-person accounts of a reasoning process, Mark Turner’s projection from The Literary Mind. Afterwards it described the process almost every time it was asked and brought the story it was trained on into most of its reasoning on 2,000 dilemmas, yet its overall decisions barely changed. Whether it learned the process or only its words is still open.

    • Fine-tuning
    • Reasoning
  2. 39/50structural twins where it raised the fable; 4 of 90 unrelated

    MARS test · 2026

    Teaching structure, not just a story

    Following Gentner’s structure mapping, a larger model generated scenarios that share a fable’s relational structure, and a small model was trained on them. One version learned to raise the fable for situations with that structure while mostly withholding it from unrelated ones; another simply named the fable for almost anything.

    • Structure mapping
    • Fine-tuning

Publications

  1. Preprint · not peer-reviewed · 2026

    Narrative Transportation and Speciesism in Large Language Models

    D. A. Arungbemi. Zenodo, 2026. Research done during Electric Sheep's Futurekind Fellowship.

    Can a story make a language model care more about an animal? Five models read a story about an animal, the same facts written plainly, or nothing, then answered a speciesism questionnaire. On the questions about that animal, their speciesism, already modest, fell to nearly zero after the story; the plain facts generally did not change it. The effects were small in absolute terms.
