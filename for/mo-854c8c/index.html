Controls for 3D–text alignment

Does a shared subspace follow the meaning of a caption, or only its wording?

Research fellow at MARS 5.0, Cambridge AI Safety Hub, fine-tuning language models on stories

The question I would bring

Your recent papers ask how far the representations of different modalities line up: 3D shapes and text in Escaping Plato’s Cave, video and text in Dynamic Reflections. In the first, raw alignment between 3D and text spaces was low, and well-chosen subspaces, which roughly separate semantic from geometric information, raised matching and retrieval a lot; its conclusion proposes such subspace analysis as a way to reveal what is unique and complementary in each modality.

I come to this from the language side. I train language models and test what training changes in them, with controls built into each test, and I would like to bring that habit to alignment scores. If the shared subspaces carry the semantic part, retrieval inside them should survive rewording a caption and break when its meaning changes. I would test this by fine-tuning one of the paper’s text encoders (BERT, RoBERTa or T5) on reworded captions and on captions with one attribute changed: does retrieval after projection follow the meaning or the wording?

  1. 12×the fable’s moral in the lens, with the fable in context

    MARS test · 2026

    Calibrating a readout with controls

    With Anthropic’s Jacobian lens, a per-layer readout of what a model is disposed to say, I looked for a story inside a model trained on it. With the story in context the readout found it plainly, the positive control; in the trained model its signal was matched by a control trained on an unrelated text, so it came from the training, not the story.

  2. 20/20times it described the process when asked

    MARS test · 2026

    Describing a reasoning process versus using it

    A model trained on first-person accounts of a reasoning process described the process almost every time it was asked and brought the story it was trained on into most of its reasoning, yet its overall decisions barely changed. Whether it learned the process or only its words is still open.

Publications

  1. Preprint · not peer-reviewed · 2026

    Narrative Transportation and Speciesism in Large Language Models

    D. A. Arungbemi. Zenodo, 2026. Research done during Electric Sheep's Futurekind Fellowship.

    Can a story make a language model care more about an animal? Five models read a story about an animal, the same facts written plainly, or nothing, then answered a speciesism questionnaire. On the questions about that animal, their speciesism, already modest, fell to nearly zero after the story; the plain facts generally did not change it. The effects were small in absolute terms.
