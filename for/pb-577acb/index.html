Measuring how readable stylised motion is

Can a model tell us which stylised motion a viewer will read best, while the animator keeps control?

Asking
A master’s research internship in Manao, and a PhD after it if the work goes well
Route
IPCVai, the Erasmus Mundus master in AI for image processing and computer vision (English), from September 2027
Now
Research fellow at MARS 5.0, Cambridge AI Safety Hub, fine-tuning language models on stories

The question I would bring

Your work brings the devices of hand-drawn animation into 3D while keeping the artist in control: SMEAR exaggerates fast motion with smear frames (elongated and multiple in-betweens, and motion lines), and your 2025 work makes smears follow an object’s trajectory. Your 2026 poster with Jean Basset and Pascal Barla, ‘How Smear Frames Improve Fast Motion Legibility’, looks at how smear frames make fast motion easier to read.

I would like to help with that question from the model side. I build in Blender and Processing and train image models, and I have learned how far a model’s score can drift from what a person sees. I would like to build ways to measure how readable a stylised motion is, using models whose scores are then checked against what viewers see, with the animator’s control left untouched. I have also worked with motion as a line: a view that redraws a recorded gesture as the path of a virtual pen, shown below.

  3. 20/20times it described the process when asked

    MARS test · 2026

    Testing what a model has learned

    In the MARS fellowship I test whether training a language model on a story changes the values it acts on, against the same story rewritten neutrally, with its ending flipped, or an unrelated text, over several seeds. One model learned to describe a reasoning process almost every time it was asked, yet its overall decisions barely changed: whether it learned the process or only its words is still open.

    • Fine-tuning

Publications

  1. Preprint · not peer-reviewed · 2026

    Narrative Transportation and Speciesism in Large Language Models

    D. A. Arungbemi. Zenodo, 2026. Research done during Electric Sheep's Futurekind Fellowship.

    Can a story make a language model care more about an animal? Five models read a story about an animal, the same facts written plainly, or nothing, then answered a speciesism questionnaire. On the questions about that animal, their speciesism, already modest, fell to nearly zero after the story; the plain facts generally did not change it. The effects were small in absolute terms.
