The question I would bring

Your work asks how designers can steer generative image models with the tools of their practice, pens and branching histories rather than one typed prompt, and how they keep their intent as iterations pile up. Your CHI 2026 workshop paper with Xiaohan Peng and Wendy Mackay names cumulative generation drift, where repeated iterations diverge from the designer’s intent, and says a system should identify when to return control to the designer.

I have built a loop that drifted away from what a person would want. A language model art-directed images from aesthetic scores, and the scores rose while the images got worse, because the human-preference check meant to undo such edits was silently failing. I would like to bring the model side to your lab: ways to measure that drift and to show it to the designer, tested with designers the way your lab tests its tools.

A loop that drifted 2026

A language model art-directs an image round by round from aesthetic scores, and a model of human preference (PickScore) undoes any edit that raises the scores but that a person would probably like less. When that check was not working, the scores rose while the images got worse.

I designed the AI of a wearable that recognises arm gestures in real time (co-author, peer-reviewed), and built a view that draws each gesture as the path of a virtual pen.

Training models and testing what changes MARS · 2026

In the MARS fellowship I test whether training a language model on a story changes the values it acts on, against matched controls. One model shifted its decisions after training on a fable, yet its reasoning never named the fable: a change its own explanations did not show.
