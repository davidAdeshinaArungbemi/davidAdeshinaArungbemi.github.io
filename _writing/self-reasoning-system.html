How do I build a system that reasons?

Well… here goes.

I will call the system Leah.

One day, I give Leah a simple problem.

A traveler visits a museum and buys 2 postcards for €1 each and 1 souvenir magnet for €3. How much does the traveler spend altogether?

Layer One — The Semantic World

First, she transforms the question into a semantic representation.

This is her way of understanding not just the words, but the structure beneath them: the objects, the quantities, the relationships, the values.

Everything she needs for reconstruction lives here.

Layer Two — The Reasoning Space

Next, Leah lifts the semantic meaning into a new form: a reasoning representation, a map inside what I call the reasoning space.

In this space lie the points of reason. I can’t fully explain them yet — they’re abstract. But once the question becomes a reasoning vector in this space, the real magic begins.

The Reasoning Core

At the center of Leah is her heart — the reasoning core. (Maybe it’d be best to use brain, but heart sounds better).

To Leah, reasoning is change.

It is simply a journey through vector space, a glide from one hyperplane to another.

The core takes the reasoning vector and shifts it,

then shifts it again,

and again

each step a small piece of thought.

Each movement edges her closer to the solution.

After several iterations, she arrives at a final point in that space.
When this point is translated back into human form, it becomes:

The traveler spends a total of five euros.

Interpretability

Fortunately, Leah’s vector transformation is not a “black-box”.

Each vector change and vector is interpretable, an unembedding layer can pull the reasoning vectors back into the semantic world, and from there into plain, human language. You can also visualise the path of reasoning.

The Idea

Reasoning is nothing more than a path through vector space.

If you can capture that path — the shift from beginning to end — you can reuse it.

Apply the same sequence of transformations to a similar problem,and you’ll land on the correct answer without running the entire system again.
