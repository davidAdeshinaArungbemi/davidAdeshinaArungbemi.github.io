Every person has a story. Not the kind you write down or tell at a party. Something more fundamental. Something you carry by existing, that moves with you through time. I am calling it a narrative.

I am not going to define what a narrative is exactly. That is intentional. I think you should bring your own life to this, your own observations. What I can do is describe how it behaves. What properties it seems to have.

Some properties

Every entity has one. You, me, the person next door. Even atoms. We will get there.

It has depth in time. A narrative is not just what you are right now. The past is in it.

It is tied to whatever it belongs to. You cannot hand your narrative to someone else. When you die, your narrative dies.

But it leaves something behind. A trace. An imprint on whatever it touched.

Now here is where I had to stop for a second. A trace can form a new narrative. Not a copy, something new. Shaped by the original but its own thing. So narratives do not transfer but they can reproduce. They leave offspring.

They compose

When two people interact, something new forms. Not just two stories running side by side. A new narrative, one that neither person had alone. A higher level narrative.

When enough of those come together you get another level above. A family. A community. A nation. Each level is genuinely new.

So: Level 1 is a single person. Level 2 forms when multiple Level 1s come together. Level 3 from multiple Level 2s. And upward.

The question I kept hitting: what actually happens when different levels meet?

The level gap

Can one person, just a Level 1, directly interact with a nation level narrative?

I do not think so. Not directly.

They just do not meet conceptually. A nation does not experience you the way a friend does. You cannot address a nation the way you address a person. There is no shared interface.

What actually happens is that the higher level projects downward through representatives. You do not meet the nation. You meet a soldier, a bureaucrat, a law. That soldier is still Level 1, still a person. But they are carrying something larger. The nation reaches you through them.

Narratives of different levels do not interact directly. They interface through projection, through representation.

It is a graph not a tree

I initially pictured this as a clean tree. Levels stacking neatly. That is wrong.

A person belongs to a family, a city, a nation, a religion, sometimes all at once. Those are not all the same level and they do not sit neatly on top of each other. It is a graph. Tangled.

And so influence does not follow a simple up or down path. It travels through edges. The structure of the graph determines how far something travels, not just the level it starts from.

A family level narrative may never directly touch a nation. But it shapes the people inside it. Those people carry that into other narratives they touch, which propagate further. Think of a child who grows up in an abusive family and goes on to advocate against the concept of family entirely. The family did not directly change anything at the top. The path was long and indirect. But the path existed.

Why going up is harder

Higher level narratives affect lower levels easily. Culture, religion, belief systems. You are born inside them. They shape you before you even know what they are. They do not ask. They are just the environment you form inside of.

Going up is much harder. A single person trying to change a nation level narrative has to actively push their idea through edges, survive each connection, reach enough people to matter at each level.

Most of the time it gets filtered out.

And the filter is not some external force. It is the graph itself. Every existing narrative that benefits from the current arrangement resists change, not through intent, but through structure. A new idea traveling upward has to survive all of that.

Most do not.

Position, not power

What I came to realise: a common person and a president are both Level 1. Structurally the same kind of thing. But a president sits at a highly connected node. Their ideas have more paths to travel through. That is why they have more effect. Not because their narrative is stronger. Because of where they sit in the graph.

Change from below is possible but it requires building the path. Finding other narratives that agree. Growing. Surviving each layer. That is what movements are. What organising is. Deliberate graph construction.

On the observer

Is a narrative objective or subjective?

Subjective. Always. It depends on whoever is observing.

Two people watching the same person live their life will describe two different narratives. Both real. Both valid from where they stand. And if the observer dies, that particular narrative of the observed dies with them, and leaves its own trace.

The observer does not have to be human. Does not even have to be conscious. In physics, observation is just interaction. An atom interacting with its environment is in some sense being observed by it. So yes, atoms have narratives. Their histories are real even without a person to witness them.

When a narrative ends

A narrative is untransferable. You can tell someone your story. But that is not your narrative. That is a retelling. Something is already lost. The lived experience cannot move intact from one person to another.

When you die, your narrative dies. When a nation collapses, that nation level narrative ends. But the people do not forget. The effects do not disappear. What remains is the trace.

The trace is not the narrative. It is an imprint. Secondhand, distorted already by whoever received it and whatever they made of it. But still enough to matter. Sometimes enough to become the seed of something new.

That is what surprised me most. Narratives do not just end. They echo. And sometimes the echo becomes its own thing.

These are working ideas, not conclusions. Use your own life to fill in what I have left open.
